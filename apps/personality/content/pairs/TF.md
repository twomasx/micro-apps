# Thinking and Feeling, together

## Both T

You both lean toward logic and frankness. You are likely to say what you think to each other and to settle a disagreement by arguing it out. Day to day, the risk is that a debate gets won while a hurt goes unnoticed, and that neither of you says the warm thing out loud.

## Both F

You both lean toward sympathy and cooperation. You are likely to be gentle with each other and quick to notice when the other is upset. Day to day, the risk is that you both avoid a hard subject to keep the peace, and that a needed criticism never gets said.

## One T, one F

One of you reaches first for the logic and the other for how people are affected. This tends to show up when one of you brings a problem home: one offers a fix, the other wanted to be heard. Frank feedback may sting more than it was meant to, and tact may be read as avoiding the point.

## Questions to talk through

- When I tell you about a problem, how can I show whether I want advice or just to be heard?
- How does each of us prefer to receive criticism?
- Is there something we have both been avoiding saying?
- What does each of us do, in practice, that the other should read as care?
