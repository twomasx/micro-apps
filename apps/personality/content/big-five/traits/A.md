# Agreeableness

## What this trait measures

This trait measures how much you attend to other people's feelings and needs, and how much you avoid conflict and pushiness. It has two parts: Compassion, which is about caring for others, and Politeness, which is about respect and restraint.

## Higher

Your answers leaned toward the higher end. You are likely to notice when someone is upset, to put other people's needs close to your own and to keep the peace when you can. You tend to give people the benefit of the doubt and to soften criticism.

## Middle

Your answers landed near the middle. You probably care about how people feel and also stand your ground when something matters to you. You may be gentle with people close to you and blunter with people who push you. Whether you give way likely depends on the stakes.

## Lower

Your answers leaned toward the lower end. You are likely to say what you think plainly, to argue your side and to put the task ahead of keeping everyone comfortable. You tend to judge a request on its merits before you agree to it. This does not mean you do not care about people.

## Higher in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to help when asked, and often before being asked. You may find it hard to say no, and you might agree to plans you would rather skip. Arguments tend to upset you, even when you are not part of them.

At work, you are likely to be a cooperative teammate who notices when a colleague is struggling. You tend to share credit and to avoid pushing your own interests hard. Asking for something for yourself, or giving blunt feedback, may feel uncomfortable.

With other people, you tend to be warm, forgiving and easy to get along with. People are likely to come to you with their problems. It can help to notice when your own needs are going unmet, and to say so before resentment builds.

## Middle in depth

In everyday life, you probably help people readily but also keep track of your own needs. You may let small things go and speak up about larger ones. Some arguments seem worth having to you, and you avoid the rest.

At work, you are likely to cooperate by default and push back when a decision seems unfair or wrong. You can usually give feedback with some care and still make the point. You may be more accommodating with some colleagues and firmer with others.

With other people, you tend to be friendly and also willing to disagree. Close friends may see your soft side more than strangers do. You may sometimes be unsure whether to keep the peace or raise a problem, and your choice is likely to depend on how much the issue matters to you.

## Lower in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to be direct about what you want and what you think. You may be skeptical of people's motives until they earn your trust. You tend not to be swayed by guilt or by requests you see as unreasonable.

At work, you are likely to argue for your position, push back on weak ideas and negotiate firmly. You tend to focus on getting the task right over keeping the mood pleasant. Decisions that need someone to say an unpopular thing may come easier to you.

With other people, you tend to be frank, and some people value knowing exactly where they stand with you. Others may hear your directness as harsh or uncaring when you did not mean it that way. Saying what you appreciate, as well as what you disagree with, can help.
