# Extraversion

## What this trait measures

This trait measures how much you seek out people, activity and attention, and how often you feel upbeat, energetic emotions. It has two parts: Enthusiasm, which is about warmth and positive emotion, and Assertiveness, which is about taking charge and speaking up.

## Higher

Your answers leaned toward the higher end. You are likely to feel more awake around people, to start conversations easily and to speak up in groups. Good moods tend to come quickly and show. A long day alone is more likely to leave you restless than rested.

## Middle

Your answers landed near the middle. You probably enjoy company and also need time alone, and the balance shifts from day to day. You may speak up in a small group you know well and stay quieter in a large group of strangers.

## Lower

Your answers leaned toward the lower end. You are likely to recover your energy in quiet, to prefer a few close people over a crowd and to stay out of the spotlight. This describes where your energy goes. It does not mean you dislike people.

## Higher in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to fill your free time with plans, calls and gatherings. You may think out loud and feel better after talking a problem through with someone. Excitement and good moods tend to come easily and show on your face.

At work, you are likely to enjoy meetings, team tasks and work that puts you in front of people. You may be the first to speak, to offer an opinion or to take the lead. Long hours of quiet work on your own may drain you.

With other people, you tend to make friends quickly and keep a wide circle. Your energy can lift a room. Quieter people may find it hard to get a word in unless you pause and ask them, and they may need more time alone than you expect.

## Middle in depth

In everyday life, you probably like a mix of social plans and quiet evenings. A busy weekend may leave you wanting a calm one, and a quiet week may leave you wanting company. You may be talkative with close friends and reserved with new people.

At work, you are likely to be comfortable both in meetings and in long stretches of focused work alone. You may take the lead when a situation calls for it without seeking the role.

With other people, you can usually match the energy of the group: lively when others are lively, calm when they are calm. Friends who are more outgoing and friends who are more reserved may both find you easy to be around. It can help to notice when you have had enough of company, or of solitude, and to say so.

## Lower in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to prefer a quiet evening, a small dinner or time on your own to a large party. Your good moods are likely to be calm rather than excited. After a busy social day, you are likely to need time alone to feel like yourself again.

At work, you are likely to do your best thinking alone or with one or two people. You may prefer writing to speaking up in a large meeting, and you tend to think before you talk. Roles that need constant contact with strangers may tire you.

With other people, you tend to keep a small circle of close friends. You are likely to listen more than you talk. Others may read your quiet as distance, so it can help to tell them when you are simply recharging.
