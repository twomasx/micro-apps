# Neuroticism

## What this trait measures

This trait measures how strongly and how often you feel negative emotions such as worry, irritation and sadness. It has two parts: Volatility, which is about anger and quick changes of mood, and Withdrawal, which is about worry, doubt and low mood.

## Higher

Your answers leaned toward the higher end. You are likely to feel worry, irritation or sadness strongly, and for longer, when things go wrong. You may notice risks early and take setbacks to heart. This describes a personality trait. It is not a diagnosis of anything.

## Middle

Your answers landed near the middle. You probably feel stress, irritation or worry when there is a clear reason, and settle again once it passes. A hard week may unsettle you, but small setbacks usually do not stay with you for long.

## Lower

Your answers leaned toward the lower end. You are likely to stay calm and steady under pressure and to recover quickly from upsets. Worry and irritation tend to pass without taking hold. You may be surprised by how much the same event bothers someone else.

## Higher in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to feel things strongly. A small criticism or a change of plans can stay on your mind for hours. You may think through what could go wrong before it happens, which can leave you well prepared and also tired from worrying.

At work, you are likely to care a lot about mistakes and about what others think of your work. Pressure, conflict and uncertain situations may feel heavy. Clear expectations and some warning before changes tend to help.

With other people, you may want more reassurance, and you may react quickly when you feel let down. You are also likely to notice signs of tension early. People close to you can help by staying steady and saying plainly where things stand. If strong feelings are making daily life hard, talking with someone you trust can help.

## Middle in depth

In everyday life, you probably feel stress when there is a real reason and settle again once it passes. A bad day can get to you, but you usually feel better by the next morning. Some situations, such as conflict or uncertainty, may affect you more than others.

At work, you are likely to stay fairly steady under normal pressure and to feel the strain when deadlines pile up or a problem drags on. You may worry about a mistake for a while, then move on.

With other people, you are likely to show some feeling when you are hurt or annoyed, and then let it go. Strong reactions can happen but are unlikely to last long. You may be the calm one in some relationships and the more anxious one in others, depending on the person.

## Lower in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to stay even-tempered when things go wrong. Delays, small losses and changed plans tend not to upset you for long. You probably spend little time worrying about what might happen.

At work, you are likely to stay calm under pressure and to keep working through setbacks. Criticism tends to feel like information rather than a blow. You may be the person others look to when a situation is tense.

With other people, you tend to be steady and slow to anger. Your calm can help others settle. People who feel things more strongly may read your calm as not caring, or feel that you play down a problem that worries them. Asking how much something matters to them, before you offer reassurance, can help.
