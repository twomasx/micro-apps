# Openness to experience

## What this trait measures

This trait measures how much you seek out and enjoy new ideas, art, imagination and unfamiliar experiences. It has two parts: Intellect, which is about ideas, and Openness, which is about beauty, feeling and imagination.

## Higher

Your answers leaned toward the higher end. You are likely to enjoy abstract ideas, to notice art, music and nature, and to go looking for things you have not tried. A routine with nothing new in it may start to feel flat to you after a while.

## Middle

Your answers landed near the middle. You probably enjoy some new ideas and experiences, but you also value the familiar and do not go out of your way for novelty. Whether you reach for something new likely depends on the subject, your mood and how much time you have.

## Lower

Your answers leaned toward the lower end. You are likely to prefer the practical and familiar over the abstract and untried. You tend to judge an idea by whether it is useful now, and long talk about theory may interest you less than getting something done.

## Higher in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to read widely, follow a topic a long way and keep a list of places, foods or music you want to try. Art, a view or a piece of music can move you strongly. You may spend a good part of the day in your own thoughts.

At work, you tend to enjoy problems without a set answer and tasks where you can try a new approach. Repeating the same steps with no room to change them is likely to wear on you. You may lose interest once a problem is solved and the rest is routine.

With other people, you tend to enjoy long conversations about ideas, beliefs and what could be. Talk that stays on practical matters may hold your interest less. People who prefer the familiar may find your steady appetite for change tiring.

## Middle in depth

In everyday life, you probably mix the familiar with the new. You might keep the same favorite places and meals but try something different now and then, often when someone else suggests it. You enjoy some art, music or ideas without making them the center of your free time.

At work, you are likely to be comfortable with both routine tasks and problems that need a fresh approach. You may prefer new methods that have already been tried somewhere else.

With other people, you can usually follow a conversation about abstract ideas and also enjoy one about everyday plans. You may act as a bridge between a friend who always wants something new and one who prefers what they know. Your interest in novelty is likely to rise and fall with the topic.

## Lower in depth

In everyday life, you are likely to know what you like and to stick with it. You may return to the same places, meals and music because they reliably please you. You tend to prefer concrete facts and plain explanations over theories and symbols.

At work, you are likely to be at ease with clear methods that are known to work. You tend to ask how a new idea will help in practice before you take it up. Changes made for their own sake may frustrate you.

With other people, you tend to prefer talk about real events and practical plans over long debates about ideas. Your tastes are likely to stay steady, which people close to you can count on. People who chase novelty may read you as cautious, and you may read them as restless.
