# Extraversion and Introversion

## What this scale measures

This scale measures where you get your energy and how much you seek out company, talk and attention. Ten questions fed it, five toward each side. Your percentage shows how far your answers went toward one side. It does not compare you with other people.

## Leaning E

Your answers leaned toward Extraversion. You are likely to feel more awake around people, to start conversations and to be at ease with attention on you. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. A long quiet day alone is more likely to leave you flat than rested.

## Leaning I

Your answers leaned toward Introversion. You are likely to recover your energy in quiet, to say less in groups and to prefer staying out of the spotlight. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. It describes where your energy goes. It does not mean you dislike people or lack social skill.

## Near the middle

Between 50% and 55% we call the scale balanced. You probably act either way depending on the day, the people and how tired you are. Many people score here. If you took the test again, one or two different answers could flip the letter, while your percentage would hardly move. Read the percentage, not the letter.

## Big Five

This scale uses questions written for the Big Five trait Extraversion. A result toward E matches higher Extraversion. A result toward I matches lower Extraversion. Researchers describe Extraversion as a continuous trait, with most people near the middle and fewer at either end.
