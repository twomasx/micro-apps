# Judging and Perceiving

## What this scale measures

This scale measures how you like to run your time and surroundings: with plans, order and things settled, or loosely, with options left open.

## Leaning J

Your answers leaned toward Judging. You are likely to make a plan and follow it, to keep things in order and to feel better once a decision is made. The name is historical. It does not mean you are judgmental.

## Leaning P

Your answers leaned toward Perceiving. You are likely to improvise, to put off closing a decision and to be untroubled by some disorder. The name is historical. It does not mean you are more perceptive than other people.

## Near the middle

You probably plan some parts of your life and leave others loose.

## Big Five

This scale uses questions written for the Big Five trait Conscientiousness, mostly its orderliness and self-discipline parts, plus two questions we wrote ourselves for the Perceiving side. A result toward J matches higher Conscientiousness. A result toward P matches lower Conscientiousness. In the research, the link between this type scale and its Big Five trait is weaker than for Extraversion and Openness, so read this scale with a little more caution.
