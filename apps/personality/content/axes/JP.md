# Judging and Perceiving

## What this scale measures

This scale measures how you like to run your time and surroundings: with plans, order and things settled, or loosely, with options left open. Ten questions fed it, five toward each side. Your percentage shows how far your answers went toward one side. It does not compare you with other people.

## Leaning J

Your answers leaned toward Judging. You are likely to make a plan and follow it, to keep things in order and to feel better once a decision is made. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. The name is historical. It does not mean you are judgmental.

## Leaning P

Your answers leaned toward Perceiving. You are likely to improvise, to put off closing a decision and to be untroubled by some disorder. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. The name is historical. It does not mean you are more perceptive than other people.

## Near the middle

Between 50% and 55% we call the scale balanced. You probably plan some parts of your life and leave others loose. Many people score here. If you took the test again, one or two different answers could flip the letter, while your percentage would hardly move. Read the percentage, not the letter.

## Big Five

This scale uses questions written for the Big Five trait Conscientiousness, mostly its orderliness and self-discipline parts, plus two questions we wrote ourselves for the Perceiving side. A result toward J matches higher Conscientiousness. A result toward P matches lower Conscientiousness. In the research, the link between this type scale and its Big Five trait is weaker than for Extraversion and Openness, so read this scale with a little more caution.
