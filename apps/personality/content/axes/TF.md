# Thinking and Feeling

## What this scale measures

This scale measures what you put first when you decide and when you deal with people: logic and frankness, or sympathy and cooperation. Ten questions fed it, five toward each side. Your percentage shows how far your answers went toward one side. It does not compare you with other people.

## Leaning T

Your answers leaned toward Thinking. You are likely to look at the logic of a problem first, to give honest criticism and to be comfortable disagreeing. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. It does not mean you have no feelings or do not care about people. It describes what you reach for first.

## Leaning F

Your answers leaned toward Feeling. You are likely to notice how others feel, to weigh the effect on people when you decide and to prefer cooperation over competition. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. It does not mean you cannot reason. It describes what you reach for first.

## Near the middle

Between 50% and 55% we call the scale balanced. You probably weigh logic and people about equally, or switch depending on what is at stake. Many people score here. If you took the test again, one or two different answers could flip the letter, while your percentage would hardly move. Read the percentage, not the letter.

## Big Five

This scale uses questions written for the Big Five trait Agreeableness, plus four questions we wrote ourselves for the Thinking side. A result toward F matches higher Agreeableness. A result toward T matches lower Agreeableness. In the research, the link between this type scale and its Big Five trait is weaker than for Extraversion and Openness, so read this scale with a little more caution.
