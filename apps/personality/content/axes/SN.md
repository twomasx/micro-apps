# Sensing and Intuition

## What this scale measures

This scale measures what kind of information you are drawn to: facts, details and known methods, or ideas, patterns and imagination. Ten questions fed it, five toward each side. Your percentage shows how far your answers went toward one side. It does not compare you with other people.

## Leaning S

Your answers leaned toward Sensing. You are likely to trust what you can see, measure and test, to prefer methods you know, and to have limited patience for abstract talk. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. It describes what you prefer to pay attention to. It says nothing about how well you think.

## Leaning N

Your answers leaned toward Intuition. You are likely to enjoy ideas for their own sake, to daydream, and to look for the pattern behind the facts. The higher the number, the more often this will be true of you. It describes what you prefer to pay attention to. It says nothing about how well you think.

## Near the middle

Between 50% and 55% we call the scale balanced. You probably move between the practical and the abstract depending on the task. Many people score here. If you took the test again, one or two different answers could flip the letter, while your percentage would hardly move. Read the percentage, not the letter.

## Big Five

This scale uses questions written for the Big Five trait Openness to experience, mostly its imagination, intellect and adventurousness parts. A result toward N matches higher Openness. A result toward S matches lower Openness. Lower Openness means a preference for the familiar and the concrete. It is not a measure of intelligence.
