# ESTJ

## Summary

You prefer company and activity, concrete facts, decisions made by logic, and a clear plan. You tend to take charge of getting things organized: who does what, by when, in what order. You trust methods that have been proven, and you say plainly when something is not working. You are likely to finish what you commit to and to expect the same from others. Open-ended discussion with no decision tends to frustrate you. People usually know where they stand with you.

## Strengths

You tend to turn a goal into steps and get people moving on them. You decide quickly once you have the facts. You hold to your commitments and to the standards you set. You usually say what you expect in plain words, which saves people from guessing.

## Blind spots

You may decide before everyone has been heard. You can see a different way of working as a wrong way. How people feel about a decision may get little weight next to whether it is efficient. You may find it hard to leave a thing unsettled when waiting would be wiser.

## At work

You tend to want clear authority, measurable goals and a team that delivers on time. You are likely to prefer a proven process over an experiment. Vague roles, missed deadlines and repeated debate over a settled question tend to frustrate you. You usually take on responsibility without being asked.

## Under stress

You may take tighter control and become more critical of others. You might feel unappreciated and suddenly take things personally, which is unlike you. Getting the facts down on paper, handing off one task and taking a real break usually help.

## How others may read you

People may see you as capable, direct and in charge. Some may find you bossy or quick to judge. Those who work with you tend to trust that you will do what you said. You may need to ask more questions and to give praise as readily as you give corrections.
