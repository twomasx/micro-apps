# ISTJ

## Summary

You prefer quiet, facts, logic and a plan. You tend to work through things alone and in order, and you trust what has been tested over what merely sounds good. When you decide, you weigh consistency before feelings, and once you commit you usually finish. You are likely to keep your word, keep records and notice when a detail is off. People may find you reserved at first. You tend to show care by being reliable more than by saying it.

## Strengths

You tend to finish what you start and to do it the way you said you would. You check facts before you rely on them, so your work usually holds up. You are comfortable working alone for long stretches. When rules are clear, you apply them the same way to everyone, including yourself.

## Blind spots

You may hold on to a method because it has worked before, even after conditions change. You can deliver a correct verdict without noticing how it lands. Ideas with no evidence yet may get dismissed too early. You may take on work quietly instead of saying you are overloaded.

## At work

You tend to want clear goals, defined roles and enough notice to prepare. You are likely to prefer written instructions over loose talk, and a quiet place over an open room. A sudden change of plan with no reason given is likely to bother you more than a heavy workload does.

## Under stress

You may tighten your routines and go quiet. You might replay what went wrong in detail, or take over tasks because doing them yourself feels safer than explaining them. Time alone with a concrete task to finish usually helps more than talking it through.

## How others may read you

People may read your reserve as coolness and your directness as criticism. Those who know you longer tend to see the steadiness behind both. You may need to say out loud that you care, because the people around you cannot always tell from your actions alone.
