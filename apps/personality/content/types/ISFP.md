# ISFP

## Summary

You prefer quiet, concrete experience, decisions guided by your values, and open options. You tend to live in what is in front of you: the people, the place, the task at hand. You are likely to be gentle with others and to dislike conflict and competition. You show what matters to you through what you do more than what you say. You tend to take things as they come and to resist being scheduled. You may keep strong feelings private.

## Strengths

You tend to notice what a person or a moment needs and to respond in a practical way. You accept people as they are and rarely push your views on them. You adjust easily when plans change. You usually put care into how things look, feel or sound, and into doing a thing well for its own sake.

## Blind spots

You may avoid a disagreement until you quietly leave the situation instead. You can put off decisions and long-term planning because they close off choices. Criticism may hit harder than the other person meant it to. You may assume people know what you value when you have never told them.

## At work

You tend to want freedom in how you do the work, a calm setting and people who are kind to each other. You are likely to prefer practical tasks with a visible result over theory. Tight schedules, public competition and blunt feedback tend to wear on you. You usually work in your own rhythm.

## Under stress

You may go quiet, pull away and become hard on yourself. You might drop plans altogether, or turn unusually critical of people you care about. Time alone doing something with your hands, and no demand to decide today, usually help.

## How others may read you

People may see you as quiet, easygoing and kind. Some may miss how strongly you hold your values, because you rarely argue for them. Those close to you tend to find you loyal in a low-key way. You may need to speak up earlier, before a small hurt becomes a reason to leave.
