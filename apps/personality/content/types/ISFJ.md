# ISFJ

## Summary

You prefer quiet, concrete facts, decisions that consider people, and a settled plan. You tend to notice what the people around you need and to take care of it without being asked. You remember details about others, such as what they like and what went wrong last time. You trust methods that have worked before. You are likely to keep commitments even when they cost you. You tend to avoid the spotlight and may find it hard to ask for help in return.

## Strengths

You tend to be dependable in small, practical ways that people come to rely on. You remember what matters to each person and act on it. You follow through on routine work that others let slide. You usually keep the peace by preparing ahead, so that fewer things go wrong in the first place.

## Blind spots

You may say yes when you mean no, then feel worn down and unseen. You can avoid a hard conversation for so long that the problem grows. A new way of doing things may feel like a risk before you have looked at it. You may wait to be noticed instead of saying what you need.

## At work

You tend to want a stable team, clear expectations and work that visibly helps someone. You are likely to prefer a known process and time to do things properly. Being rushed, or being asked to deliver criticism in public, is likely to sit badly with you. You usually do more behind the scenes than you report.

## Under stress

You may keep working harder and say less. Small worries about what could go wrong can multiply, and you might take other people's moods as your fault. Quiet time, a familiar routine and one person who asks how you are usually help.

## How others may read you

People may see you as quiet, kind and easy to lean on. Some may assume you have no strong opinions because you do not push them. Those close to you tend to learn that you notice a great deal and remember it. You may need to state your limits plainly, because others will not always guess them.
