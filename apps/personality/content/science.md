# The science

## What this test measures

This test asks 40 questions and reports four scales. Each scale is built from questions written to measure one of the Big Five personality traits, the model most used in personality research. We report the results with type letters because many people know them.

- E or I comes from questions about Extraversion.
- S or N comes from questions about Openness to experience.
- T or F comes from questions about Agreeableness.
- J or P comes from questions about Conscientiousness.

A 1989 study by McCrae and Costa found that the four type scales line up with these four traits. The link was strong for E/I and S/N and moderate for T/F and J/P. The fifth Big Five trait, Neuroticism, has no type letter and this test does not measure it.

## Why you get percentages

Types suggest two kinds of people on each scale. The data does not show that. On each trait most people score near the middle and fewer score at the ends. Most people are near the middle on at least one scale.

That is why we show a percentage for each scale. When your winning side is 55% or less, we label the scale "balanced". The letter still appears in your four-letter code, but it tells you little.

## How stable your result is

Each scale has 10 questions. One answer changed by one step moves that scale by 2.5 percentage points. If you are near 50%, one or two different answers on another day can change your letter. Your percentage will have barely moved.

So a different four-letter code on a retest does not mean you changed. Compare the percentages.

## Where the questions come from

Thirty-two questions come from the International Personality Item Pool (IPIP), a public-domain collection used in research. We added "I" to the start of each one.

We wrote the other eight questions ourselves: four for Thinking, two for Sensing and two for Perceiving. The IPIP questions on those sides mostly describe faults instead of preferences. Our eight questions have not been validated yet. We will check how well they work once enough people have taken the test.

## What this test is not

It is not a clinical tool. It cannot diagnose anything. It was not built for hiring and should not be used to choose between people. It does not measure intelligence or ability, and it cannot tell you which job or partner is right for you.

## About comparing two people

The compare page shows how similar two sets of answers are. Research on couples finds that having similar personalities predicts relationship satisfaction only weakly. A large study across three countries found that similarity added very little once each partner's own traits were counted.
